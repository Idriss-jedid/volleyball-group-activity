Abstract
Group activity recognition asks what a team is doing rather than what each person does. In a volleyball rally the answer — a set, a spike, a pass or a won point, by the left or the right team — depends on what each of the twelve players does and on which side of the net they are.

This work reimplements the hierarchical deep temporal model of Ibrahim et al. (CVPR 2016), in which a first LSTM describes each player over time and a second LSTM recognizes the team activity from the pooled players, in three versions. V1 reproduces the paper faithfully, with AlexNet features and the original protocol: the full model reaches 85.9% test accuracy against the 81.9% reported, and every conclusion of the paper's ablation holds. V2 is the modernized version of the course built by the paper's author, with a ResNet50 backbone: the two-stage model with one representation per team reaches 91.8%. A preliminary analysis of the labels explains why team pooling matters: the player actions predict the kind of activity with 94% accuracy, but the side only becomes recoverable, at 89%, once the two teams are pooled separately.

V3 extends the model with an actor transformer that attends over every player at every frame and an end-to-end network whose ConvNeXt-T backbone is trained with both the team loss and the player-action loss (92.7%). Optuna hyper-parameter search, mirror test-time augmentation and seed and model ensembles bring the final accuracy to 93.9% (macro-F1 94.3) on the 1,337 test clips. To remove the need for ground-truth boxes, nine COCO detectors — YOLO11, YOLOv8, RT-DETR, Faster R-CNN and RetinaNet — and a YOLO11 detector fine-tuned on the players replace the annotated tracks: with the fine-tuned detector, the end-to-end model reaches 92.3% from raw frames, 0.4 points below the annotated tracks.

All models are trained on Kaggle T4 GPUs from a single configuration-driven code base, with video-level splits, selection on the validation set only, and every number reported on the official test split.
